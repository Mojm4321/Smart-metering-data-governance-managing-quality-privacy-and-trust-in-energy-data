# Smart Metering Data Governance: Managing Quality, Privacy And Trust In Energy Data
## By Jolly Madamedon

# Introduction & Context
Smart meters are digital devices that record household eletricity consumption and transmit usage data at regular intervals. This enables more accurate billing, improved visibility of energy use and greater opreational efficiency across the energy system. In the UK, the smart meter programme is supplier-led, supported by government policy (originally overseen by BEIS) and regulated by Ofgem. The programme aims to modernise the national energy infastructure, reduce inefficiences and support more cost-effective energy use for households over the long term.

The UK Government has strongly supported data driven innovation within the energy industry, aiming to 'ensure that every home and business was offered a smart meter by 2020, delivered as cost-effectively as possible'. However smart meters generate highly granular household data, introducing significant data governance challenges. Energy consumption data can reveal personal and sensitive insights about individual behaviour, such as occupancy patterns, daily routines, socioeconomic status and entertainment preferences creating risks related to privacy, consent, data retention, data minmisation, security and proportionality.

Without clear governance controls, these risks threaten public trust, weaken regulatory compliance, and expose organisations to reputational, financial and legal consequences over the long term.
