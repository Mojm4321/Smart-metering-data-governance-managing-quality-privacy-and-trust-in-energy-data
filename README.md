# Smart Metering Data Governance: Managing Data Quality, Privacy And Trust 
## By Jolly Madamedon 
##(Analysis based on a case study produced for the British Academy and the Royal Society)

# Background
Smart meters are digital devices that record household electricity consumption and transmit usage data at regular intervals. This enables more accurate billing, improved visibility of energy use and greater operational efficiency across the energy system. In the UK, the smart meter programme is supplier-led, supported by government policy (originally overseen by BEIS) and regulated by Ofgem. The programme aims to modernise the national energy infrastructure, reduce inefficiences and support more cost-effective energy use for households over the long term.

The UK Government has strongly supported data driven innovation within the energy industry, aiming to 'ensure that every home and business was offered a smart meter by 2020, delivered as cost-effectively as possible'. However smart meters generate highly granular household data, introducing significant data governance challenges. Energy consumption data can reveal personal and sensitive insights about individual behaviour, such as occupancy patterns, daily routines, socioeconomic status and entertainment preferences creating risks related to privacy, consent, data retention, data minimisation, security and proportionality.

Without clear governance controls, these risks threaten public trust, weaken regulatory compliance, and expose organisations to long term reputational, financial and legal consequences and limit the value that can be derived from advanced analytics and automation. Although the governments have shown strong interest in deploying cutting edge data technologies, governance mechanisms must evolve equally, particularly in the case of a one-off, national roll-out of an infrastructure rollout. 

Failure to do so risks limiting consumer feedback loops that could drive rapid service improvements, while also increasing the likelihood of widespread disruptions that place sustained pressure on technical systems and both online and field-based operational teams.

Smart metering data governance is therefore essential to define clear rules, roles and controls governing how energy consumption data is collected, accessed, shared, stored and retained or deleted. Effective governance ensures that smart meter data is used responsibly, lawfully and transparently, balancing the benefits of advanced data use with the protection of individual rights. This case study examines the key governance challenges and opportunities associated with smart metering data by analysing privacy, security, consent and proportionality risks, and demonstrating how governance frameworks- supported by technical controls and institutional oversight, can enable innovation and operational value while managing ethical, legal and societal risk.
